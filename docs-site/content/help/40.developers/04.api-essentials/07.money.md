The API never uses floating-point money. Every amount is an integer in the currency's minor unit, sent with its currency code, and rounding follows one rule.

## Send amounts in minor units

Every amount in the API is an integer in the currency's minor unit, paired with an ISO 4217
currency code. You will not find a floating-point amount anywhere.

| Amount | You send | Currency |
|---|---|---|
| $19.99 | `1999` | `USD` |
| ₹499.00 | `49900` | `INR` |
| ¥1,200 | `1200` | `JPY` |

`JPY` has no minor unit, so `1200` means ¥1,200 rather than ¥12. tmbilling knows each currency's
exponent. Your code only needs it when formatting for display.

::warning{title="Don't convert to a float at the boundary"}
Parsing `1999` into a float, dividing by 100 and sending it back is how you lose a cent. Keep the
integer, and divide only in your formatter at the moment of display.
::

## Expect half-up rounding, per line

tmbilling rounds half-up to the nearest minor unit. That matters wherever a calculation lands on a
fraction: percentage discounts, proration for a partial period, tax on a line, or a per-unit price
times a quantity.

It rounds each line, then sums the rounded lines — rather than rounding one unrounded total. That
is why an invoice still adds up when your customer checks it by hand.

## Match currencies across the hierarchy

You set a currency at several levels, and they have to agree:

| Level | Field | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| Business | `default_currency` | The currency the business bills in by default, and the one its reporting rolls up to. |
| Price | `currency` | The one currency a price is denominated in. Sell in three currencies and you create three prices. |
| Customer | `currency` | The currency you bill this customer in. It decides which prices you can put them on. |
| Subscription | derived | Every item on one subscription shares one currency. tmbilling refuses a mixed-currency item list rather than converting it. |

tmbilling never converts currency when billing. Conversion exists only for reporting: give a
business exchange rates and its metrics roll up into its default currency. That never changes what
you charge a customer.

## Handle zero amounts

A zero-amount invoice is normal — a fully discounted period, or a trial that produced a document
but no charge. tmbilling posts it and marks it paid without calling a gateway.

Invoice amounts are never negative. Reduce what a customer owes with a discount line, a credit
application or a credit note. Each is its own object with its own ledger entries, so you keep the
reason for the reduction. See [Credits and the ledger](/help/developers/concepts/credits).
