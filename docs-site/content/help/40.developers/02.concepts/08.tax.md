You configure tax per business, and tmbilling computes it per invoice line.

## Place of supply

The place of supply decides where a sale is taxed: your customer's country, and their state or
province where the regime is sub-national, read against where you are registered.

That is why a customer's address is a tax input rather than a shipping detail.

## Configure a tax region

A region is a country you have made taxable:

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/tax_regions"}
::
::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/tax_regions"}
::

Each region holds its regime, whether your prices are inclusive or exclusive, your registration,
and the gates that switch tax off entirely — reverse charge, export and special economic zones.

### Set the component vector

A region's tax is a vector of components, not a single percentage. A domestic Indian sale carries
CGST and SGST as separate components, while an inter-state sale carries IGST instead. Both print
separately, because that is what the document has to show.

::endpoint{method="PUT" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/tax_regions/{txg}/components"}
::

Sets the component vector for one state scope.

::endpoint{method="PUT" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/tax_regions/{txg}/state_registrations"}
::

Records your registration in one state — in India, that state's GSTIN — for your own records.
The domestic and inter-state split comes from the region's home state compared with the buyer's
billing state. The GSTIN printed on an invoice is the business's `tax_registration_number` (or the
organization's, when the organization issues invoices). See
[India GST invoices](/help/developers/recipes/india-gst-invoices).

## Create a tax profile

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/tax_profiles"}
::

A profile holds the rates for one class of product, which is how a digital service and a physical
good get taxed differently in the same region. Give each product a `tax_code` to select its
profile.

## Select inclusive or exclusive pricing

Set `tax_behavior` on each price:

| Behaviour | `amount_minor` means | Your customer pays |
|---|---|---|
| `exclusive` | the net amount | More than the list price — tmbilling calculates tax and adds it. |
| `inclusive` | the total amount | Exactly the list price — tmbilling extracts the tax from it, and the net is what remains. |

::note{title="Inclusive tax comes out of one combined rate"}
For an inclusive line, tmbilling derives the components from the single combined rate. Backing
each component out of the gross separately does not reconstitute the original amount.
::

## Handle withholding

Some jurisdictions require your buyer to withhold tax and remit it directly.

Set `withholding_rate_bps` on the customer. It reduces what they pay you without reducing what
they owe: the invoice records the full amount and the withheld portion separately, so the
difference is explained rather than looking like a short payment.

## Apply exemptions

Set `tax_exempt` on a customer to switch tax off for them.

`vat_number` drives reverse charge where the regime provides for it: tmbilling zero-rates the sale
and the obligation moves to your buyer. Validate the number rather than trusting it:

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/customers/{cus}/validate_vat"}
::

## Expect each invoice to snapshot its tax context

When tmbilling issues an invoice, it snapshots your registration, the place of supply and the
rates that applied.

So changing a customer's address, or a region's rates, does not re-tax a document that already
exists. New invoices use your new configuration.
