tmbilling nests four levels, and each one has a job:

```text
Organization          your tmbilling account — API keys, webhook endpoints, the isolation boundary
└── Business          one billing entity — its own catalog, currencies, gateways, invoice numbering
    └── Customer      the payer — contacts, payment methods, balances
        └── Subscription   what they are billed for
```

## Organization

Your organization is your account. It holds your API keys and webhook endpoints, and it is the
isolation boundary: everything a key reaches belongs to that organization, enforced in the
database rather than in whichever query happens to run.

Most integrations use one organization and never think about this level again.

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/me"}
::

## Business

One organization holds many businesses, and they are genuinely independent. Each business has its
own:

- catalog of products, prices and coupons
- currencies, with its own default
- gateway accounts, the payment providers it charges through
- invoice numbering, branding and email templates
- tax configuration
- settings: dunning schedule, collection defaults, billing anchors

Create separate businesses when you sell under separate legal entities, in separate regions, or
under separate brands.

Use one business when the same entity sells several things. The catalog already handles that, and
splitting it splits your reporting too.

::note{title="Every business has a test mode and a live mode"}
They are two businesses with two ids: the live one carries `test_business_id`, the test one
carries `live_business_id`. `POST /v1/businesses` creates both and returns the one that matches
your key's mode. Each mode has its own customers, invoices and settings, and a key reaches one
mode only, so a test key cannot touch live data by accident. See
[Test and live mode](/help/developers/getting-started/test-and-live).
::

Almost every URL in the API is scoped to a business:

```text
/v1/businesses/{biz}/customers
/v1/businesses/{biz}/invoices
/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions
```

## Customer

Your customer is the payer within one business. Someone who buys from two of your businesses is
two customers, with separate invoices, balances and payment methods — because those belong to the
selling entity.

A customer owns contacts, vaulted payment methods with primary and backup roles, and three
per-currency balances. See [Customers](/help/developers/concepts/customers).

## Subscription

A subscription belongs to a customer and holds many items: one line per price, each with a
quantity. That is how a plan, three add-ons and a metered usage line share one renewal date and
one invoice.

See [Subscriptions](/help/developers/concepts/subscriptions).

## Object ids

tmbilling uses two id styles on purpose:

| Style | Looks like | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Generated | `cus_…`, `inv_…`, `sub_…`, `biz_…` | Customer-facing objects get a stable public id, separate from the internal key. Store it and expose it safely. |
| Slug | `pro`, `pro-monthly-usd`, `WELCOME20` | Catalog and coupon objects — product groups, products, prices, coupons, coupon sets — take a readable id you set. It is unique per business and immutable. Omit it and tmbilling derives one from the name. |

Slug ids are what let one configuration reference the same objects in your test and live
businesses. `pro-monthly-usd` means the same thing in both, so your deployment script, your
fixtures and your support team can all name it.
