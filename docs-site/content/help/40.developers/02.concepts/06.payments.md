tmbilling decides what to charge. A gateway does the charging.

The engine never imports a provider's SDK directly — each provider sits behind one adapter
interface. So adding a provider does not touch billing logic, and billing logic never comes to
depend on one provider's quirks.

## Configure gateway accounts

Give a business as many gateway accounts as you need. Each carries encrypted credentials, the
currencies it handles and the payment method types it supports, and is `live` or `test` to match
its business.

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/gateway_accounts"}
::

## Know how tmbilling routes a charge

For each charge, the router selects an account by currency and method type — or uses the
`gateway_account_id` you pass explicitly.

::warning{title="tmbilling never falls back silently"}
Leave a currency unmapped and tmbilling refuses the charge rather than routing it to whichever
account happens to be configured. You would otherwise discover EUR charged through a USD account
weeks later, in a reconciliation.
::

## Store payment methods

The gateway vaults the card and returns a token. tmbilling stores that token and the non-secret
descriptors.

Each method carries a status that an expiry sweep keeps current — `valid`, `expiring`, `expired`,
`invalid` or `pending_verification` — and a role of `primary`, `backup` or `none`. When the
primary declines, tmbilling falls through to the backups within the same attempt. Only a hard
decline marks a card `invalid`. Hosted checkout charges only the card your buyer entered or picked.

## Handle 3-D Secure with payment intents

When a card needs your buyer to authenticate, the charge becomes a two-step flow:

::steps

### Create an intent

`POST /payment_intents` with a purpose of `payment` or `setup`. You get back a `next_action`
describing what your buyer has to do.

### Let your buyer authenticate

They complete the provider's own flow in their browser. Your key is never involved.

### Consume the intent

`POST /payment_intents/{pi}/consume` turns the authorized intent into the actual charge.

::

An off-session charge that returns `requires_action` has nobody to ask, so tmbilling falls through
to dunning and reaches your customer by email instead.

tmbilling sweeps unconsumed intents hourly and expires them.

## Read the transaction log

tmbilling writes every gateway interaction as a transaction row — authorizations, payments and
refunds — with the gateway's own `error_code`, its id at the gateway, and a status vocabulary that
uses the same `succeeded` / `failed` words as payments and covers the awkward cases: `timeout`,
`needs_attention` and `late_failed`. The older spellings `success`, `failure` and `late_failure`
are still accepted on filters and on reconcile, and are never returned.

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/transactions"}
::

::note{title="Every refusal books an attempt"}
A failed payment is a row you can list, filter and reconcile. That is what makes "why was this
customer never charged?" a question you can answer.
::

### Reconcile late failures

Providers sometimes settle asynchronously, and sometimes reverse a success hours later.

tmbilling ingests inbound provider webhooks and reconciles them against the transaction. It
settles an in-progress charge, and a late failure reopens the invoice it had marked paid.

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/gateway_webhooks"}
::
::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/gateway_webhooks/{id}/replay"}
::

tmbilling deduplicates inbound webhooks by the provider's event id, so a provider that delivers
twice never settles twice.

## Refund a payment

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/payments/{pay}/refund"}
::

Refund in full or in part, against the original payment. tmbilling writes the refund as its own
transaction with its own ledger entries and leaves the original payment untouched.

To refund money collected on a paid invoice, use a refundable credit note instead. It keeps the
invoice, the credit and the refund tied together. See [Invoices](/help/developers/concepts/invoices).

## React to disputes

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/disputes"}
::

tmbilling surfaces chargebacks raised at the provider as first-class objects with their own
events, so your integration can react to a dispute rather than you finding it in a provider
dashboard.
