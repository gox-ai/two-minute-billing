Each business has its own catalog, with three levels:

```text
Product group        an optional grouping for reporting and portal display
└── Product          the thing you sell — a plan, an add-on, or a one-off charge
    └── Price        one way to charge for it — a currency, an interval, a model
```

## Products

Give each product a `kind`:

| Kind | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `plan` | The main thing a subscription is for. Most subscriptions carry exactly one. |
| `addon` | Something bought alongside a plan, such as extra seats or premium support. |
| `charge` | A one-off, rather than a recurring line. |

```bash
curl -X POST $TMBILLING_URL/v1/businesses/$BIZ/products \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMBILLING_API_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "name": "Pro", "code": "pro", "kind": "plan" }'
```

`DELETE` archives a product by setting `active=false` rather than removing it, so your history
keeps pointing at something real.

## Prices

Give a product as many prices as you need. Each price fixes a currency, an interval and a model.
Selling one plan monthly and yearly, in USD and EUR, means four prices on one product.

```bash
curl -X POST $TMBILLING_URL/v1/businesses/$BIZ/prices \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMBILLING_API_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "product": "pro",
    "id": "pro-monthly-usd",
    "name": "Monthly USD",
    "model": "flat",
    "currency": "USD",
    "amount_minor": 2900,
    "interval": "month"
  }'
```

Set `type` to `recurring`, the default, or `one_time`. Recurring prices need an `interval` of
`day`, `week`, `month` or `year`, plus an optional `interval_count` — so "every three months" is
`interval: "month"` with `interval_count: 3`.

### Select a pricing model

| Model | You set | How it charges |
|---|---|---|
| `flat` | `amount_minor` | A fixed amount per period, whatever the quantity. |
| `per_unit` | `amount_minor` | The amount times the quantity. Use it for seats. |
| `tiered` | `tiers[]` | Graduated. Each tier's units charge at that tier's rate, so a quantity spanning three tiers pays all three. |
| `volume` | `tiers[]` | Charges the whole quantity at the rate of the tier it lands in. |
| `stairstep` | `tiers[]` | Charges the tier's `flat_amount_minor`, wherever in the tier the quantity falls. |
| `package` | `amount_minor`, `package_size` | Sells in blocks — 1,000 credits for $10 — rounding up to whole packages. |
| `usage` | `usage_aggregation` | Meters usage and bills in arrears for the period just closed. See [Usage-based billing](/help/developers/recipes/usage-based-billing). |
| `percentage` | `percentage_bps` | Takes a cut in basis points, where `250` means 2.5%. `free_units` exempts a base amount first. |

The tier, package and percentage models bill recorded usage instead of the subscription quantity
when you give them a `usage_aggregation`. The price object then reads `metered: true`. See
[Usage-based billing](/help/developers/recipes/usage-based-billing).

### Reprice without repricing your customers

A `PATCH` on a price applies to new subscriptions only. Existing subscriptions keep the price you
created them with, so raising a price never silently reprices everyone on it.

To move existing customers, change their subscription items. See
[Upgrades and downgrades](/help/developers/recipes/plan-changes).

::note{title="Set your own price ids"}
`id` is a slug you set, such as `pro-monthly-usd`, unique per business. Omit it and tmbilling
derives one from the name, or from the currency, interval and model.

You cannot change it once set, and a collision returns `409`. That immutability is what lets you
use the same id in your test and live businesses.
::

## Add-ons

Restrict a product with `kind: "addon"` to specific plans, and give it a different price depending
on the plan it attaches to.

::endpoint{method="PUT" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/products/{prod}/applicable_plans"}
::

Restricts the add-on to a list of plans. tmbilling refuses to add it to any other plan.

::endpoint{method="PUT" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/products/{prod}/differential_prices"}
::

Sets a differential price: what the add-on costs on that particular plan, overriding its own
price. Use it to bundle an add-on cheaply with an expensive plan.

## Coupons

Coupons live in the catalog too, because they are configuration rather than transactions.

- A **coupon** is a reusable discount — percent or amount, with validity dates and constraints on
  which products it applies to.
- A **coupon set** is a batch of unique single-use codes from one definition, for a campaign where
  each recipient gets their own code.

See [Coupons and discounts](/help/developers/recipes/coupons).
