To upgrade or downgrade a subscription, you change its items. tmbilling prorates the difference, and you decide whether the change applies now or at the end of the term.

## Select `change` or `update_for_items`

Two endpoints change a subscription's items. They behave differently.

| Endpoint | You send | tmbilling does |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /subscriptions/{sub}/change` | a delta | Applies your additions, removals and quantity changes. Leaves every item you don't mention untouched. |
| `POST /subscriptions/{sub}/update_for_items` | the complete item list | Makes the subscription match your list exactly. Removes anything you left out. |

::caution{title="Send a delta to update_for_items and you delete the rest"}
Send one item to `update_for_items` on a subscription with a plan and three add-ons, and you
keep that one item and credit the other four. If you mean "change this one thing", use `change`.
::

## Upgrade a subscription

Swap one price for another in a single call:

```bash
curl -X POST $TMBILLING_URL/v1/businesses/$BIZ/subscriptions/$SUB/change \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMBILLING_API_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "items": [
      { "price": "pro-monthly-usd", "quantity": 1 },
      { "price": "starter-monthly-usd", "remove": true }
    ]
  }'
```

## Understand the proration

tmbilling prorates both sides of the change and writes each as a real invoice line with its own
ledger entries:

::steps

### Credit the unused portion

You get a credit for the removed item's remaining time in the current period, pro rata.

### Charge the new item

tmbilling charges the new item pro rata, from now to the end of the current period.

### Net it into an invoice

An upgrade usually nets to a charge. A downgrade usually nets to a credit.

::

The renewal date does not move. Your customer stays on their existing term.

::note{title="A downgrade credits the balance, it does not refund the card"}
A downgrade that nets to a credit adds that credit to the customer's balance, and tmbilling
applies it to their next invoice. To refund the money instead, issue a refundable credit note —
the two have different tax and accounting consequences. See
[Credits and the ledger](/help/developers/concepts/credits).
::

## Estimate before you charge

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions/{sub}/estimate"}
::

Send the same body you would send to `change`. You get back the exact proration, tax and credit
application, and tmbilling changes nothing.

Show that number to your customer first. It is what lets you say "you pay $18.34 today, then
$49.00 monthly from 14 October" and be right.

## Change a quantity

Adding or removing seats uses the same endpoint:

```bash
curl -X POST $TMBILLING_URL/v1/businesses/$BIZ/subscriptions/$SUB/change \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMBILLING_API_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "items": [{ "price": "seats-monthly-usd", "quantity": 25 }] }'
```

Increase the quantity and tmbilling prorates a charge for the new seats. Decrease it and you get
a prorated credit for the ones you removed.

## Schedule a downgrade for the end of the term

Apply upgrades immediately — your customer wants what they just paid for. Schedule downgrades for
the end of the term instead. Your customer has already paid for this period, so removing their
access now while keeping the money creates a support ticket.

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions/{sub}/schedule"}
::

```bash
curl -X POST $TMBILLING_URL/v1/businesses/$BIZ/subscriptions/$SUB/schedule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMBILLING_API_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "type": "change",
    "effective_at": "2026-10-14T00:00:00Z",
    "items": [{ "price": "starter-monthly-usd", "quantity": 1 }]
  }'
```

A scheduled change is a real object. List it, show your customer "your plan changes on 14
October", and withdraw it if they change their mind:

::endpoint{method="GET" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions/{sub}/scheduled_changes"}
::
::endpoint{method="DELETE" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/scheduled_changes/{sch}"}
::

## Add or remove a single item

To change one add-on, use the narrower endpoints instead of sending a whole delta:

::endpoint{method="POST" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions/{sub}/items"}
::
::endpoint{method="DELETE" path="/v1/businesses/{biz}/subscriptions/{sub}/items/{item}"}
::

Both prorate the same way as `change`.

## Reprice a plan

Editing a price affects new subscriptions only. Existing subscribers keep the price they signed
up on, so a price edit cannot silently reprice your customer base.

To move existing customers, create the new price and change their subscriptions onto it. Plan
that as a migration with a notice period, not as a `PATCH`.
