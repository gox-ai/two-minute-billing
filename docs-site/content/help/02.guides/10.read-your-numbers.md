Two Minute Billing reports revenue, MRR, ARR, churn and subscriber mix from its own records, per currency and per business. This page explains how each number is calculated and what it leaves out on purpose.

Open **Metrics** for the whole organization, or **Home** inside a business.

## Reports follow your billing models

A business can bill in four ways: **subscriptions**, **one-time sales**, **usage** and **credits**.
Two Minute Billing checks the last 90 days to see which ones the business uses. Reports for a
model the business does not use are hidden:

| Model | Reports it shows |
|---|---|
| Subscriptions | **Cancellations**, the subscription dashboards, MRR and churn |
| One-time sales | **Reports → Sales**. See [Read your sales report](/help/guides/read-your-sales) |
| Usage | **Reports → Usage** |
| Credits | **Reports → Credits** |

If you open a report for a model the business does not use, by a link for example, the page says
the business has no data of that kind in the last 90 days. It shows data when the business has
some. **Customers**, **Recovery**, **Checkout**, **Portal** and **Emails** always show. On **Customers**, a segment shows **Revenue billed, 12 months** for every type of revenue, and shows MRR beside it only when the business has subscriptions. On
**Portal**, the plan-change and cancellation blocks show only when the business has
subscriptions. On **Checkout**, the **Product** filter lists every product a checkout link can
sell, not only plans.

**Stats** and **Explore** group their measures by model: **All models**, **Subscriptions**,
**One-time sales**, **Usage**, **Credits** and **Payments**. Measures for a model the business
does not use are folded into **Not used by this business**.

### Dashboards

**Reports → Dashboards** opens on a built-in dashboard. There is one for each model, and one that
fits every business:

- **Revenue** shows revenue billed by type, gross revenue and its deductions, cash collected,
  billed customers, revenue per customer, refunds, receivables, revenue by product, paying
  customers and lifetime revenue per customer. A business with no subscriptions opens on this dashboard.
- **Business overview**, **What moved it** and **Lifecycle** show only for a business with
  subscriptions.
- **One-time sales**, **Usage** and **Credits** show for a business that uses that model.

### Usage

**Reports → Usage** was called **Usage margin**. It still opens at the same address. It shows
**Customers who stopped** (no usage in 30 days, after some before), **Units by meter** and the
**Top consumers**. For a business with a minimum commitment on a subscription, it also shows
**Commitment pacing** and **Commitment shortfall billed**. Pacing compares the fees of the term
with the commitment due so far. The fees are what is invoiced for the term plus the usage so far
that is not invoiced yet, at the subscription's prices. A subscription with a volume or stairstep
usage price shows **Pace shown at term end**: its usage amount depends on the total at the end of
the term, so only invoiced fees count until then. Margin is a section at the foot of the
page. See [Bill from the AI model price list](/help/guides/usage-and-ai-billing/model-price-list).

### Credits

**Reports → Credits** shows deferred credit revenue by month, a **Burn rate and runway** section,
**Credit packs** and refused usage. Runway puts each customer in a band: 7 days or less, 8 to 30
days, 31 to 90 days, more than 90 days, or no use in the last 7 days. The **Credit packs**
section shows **Packs sold**, **Pack sales billed** and **Revenue recognized**. **Pack sales billed**
counts a pack as revenue when it is sold. **Revenue recognized** counts it as the customer uses
the credit. Credit in a unit with no money value shows in its own units, in **Credit with no
money value** and in the burn rate. Runway counts each unit in its own units. See
[Prepaid credits](/help/guides/usage-and-ai-billing/prepaid-credits).

## The organization home

The organization totals show the same four cards as a business Home, added across all live
businesses: **MRR**, **Paying customers**, **Churn, 30 days** and **Trial conversion, 30 days**.
Each card has the same figures, change and notes as on a business. The two churn rates are
shares of customers and of recurring revenue, each divided once over all businesses.

Below them, **Revenue, 30 days** is in a row of its own. It shows what the businesses billed,
before tax, less discounts and credits, the change from the 30 days before, and the split by type: subscriptions, one-time,
usage and credit packs. Only the types with revenue show.

If no business has subscriptions, the totals show **Revenue, 30 days** and **Paying customers**.
Here, paying customers are everyone who paid, for any kind of revenue.

Each business has a card, sorted by its revenue in the last 30 days. A business with subscriptions
shows its MRR and, under it, its **other revenue, 30 days**. A business without subscriptions shows
its revenue in the last 30 days, with its orders, usage and credit use. **Needs attention** shows
**No price** only when a business has no price at all. A plan or a one-time charge is a price.

## The business home

The setup steps at the top of a new business end with two checks: the business has at least one
price, a plan or a charge, and it has billed at least once, with a subscription or an invoice.
Select the **×** to close the steps. This closes them for that business in that browser only.

A business with subscriptions and other models shows its MRR first. A second row of cards, for
**Orders**, **Usage revenue** and **Credit balance**, comes under it.

## Gross and net revenue

Revenue is net. It is what you invoiced before tax, less discounts, promotional credit, plan
credits and credit notes. Money that the customer paid is not taken off: an overpayment kept as
credit, a paid top-up or refundable credit counts as revenue.

- **Promotional credit** is free credit that paid part of an invoice. It is not revenue.
- **Plan credits** come with a plan. The plan fee is already revenue, so the usage they pay for
  does not count again.
- **Paid with prepaid credit** is usage that a credit pack paid for. The pack counts as revenue
  when you sell it, so the usage counts one time only, at what the customer paid for the pack.
- **Credit notes** count in the period you create them, so a period can show negative revenue.

On Home, the **Revenue mix** card shows the breakdown: **Gross revenue**, each deduction the
period has, and **Net revenue**. Gross revenue less the deductions is net revenue, for each
currency, revenue type and product. In **Explore** and the **Revenue** dashboard, use
**Gross revenue** and **Revenue deductions** to see the same figures over time.

MRR does not change. Credit pays an invoice, but it does not change the price of a subscription.

## What the headline numbers mean

| Metric | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Revenue, 30 days** | What the business billed in the last 30 days, before tax, less discounts and credits, whatever the billing model |
| **Paying customers** | Customers who paid in the last 30 days, for any kind of revenue |
| **MRR** | Committed recurring revenue per month |
| **ARR** | MRR annualised |
| **ARPU** | Average revenue per active subscriber |
| **Active** | Subscriptions currently billing |
| **In trial** | Started, not yet charged |
| **Cancelled** | Ended in the period |
| **Logo churn** | The share of customers lost over the window, by count rather than by value |
| **Subscription lifetime value** | Average revenue per customer times the average customer lifetime. It uses subscriptions only, and shows only after a customer has fully left |
| **Lifetime revenue per customer** | What a customer has paid you so far, for every type of revenue |

::note{title="MRR excludes usage and one-off charges"}
It is *committed* recurring revenue: what a customer is contracted to pay each month. Metered
usage and one-off charges are real revenue but not committed, so including them would make MRR
move with consumption and stop being a planning number.

Look at **Revenue, 30 days**, invoices and transactions for total billed and collected.
::

## Multiple currencies

MRR is shown per currency, because ₹1 and $1 are not the same number and adding them is how a
dashboard starts lying.

If you want one roll-up figure, set reporting exchange rates under **Settings → Currencies**. Those
rates only affect reporting — they never change what a customer is charged.

## Time zones

Each business counts its days in its own time zone (**Settings → Business profile → Time zone**).
An organization total adds each business as that business reports itself, at its own date. So the
organization figures are always the sum of the business pages, also when your businesses are in
different time zones.

## What to watch

**Past due** is the money you have earned and not collected. It should be small and it should move
— a number that only grows means your retry schedule or your emails are not working. See
[Recover failed payments](/help/guides/recover-failed-payments).

**Cancellations with reasons** tell you why customers leave, but only if your team records a reason
when they cancel. It costs one click and it is the difference between knowing and guessing.
**Reports → Cancellations** leads with what each cancellation cost: MRR lost by month or week, why
paying customers left, how long they stayed, the reasons given and the largest losses. Trials that
never converted are counted separately, so they don't inflate your paying churn.

**Trials not converting** shows up as a healthy in-trial count and flat active count.

## Going deeper

Every list — customers, subscriptions, invoices, transactions — can be filtered and sorted, and
the numbers on the metrics screens link through to the rows behind them. When a figure looks
wrong, click it and read the underlying records rather than re-deriving it yourself.
