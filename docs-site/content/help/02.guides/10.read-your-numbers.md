## What the headline numbers mean

| Metric | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **MRR** | Committed recurring revenue per month |
| **ARR** | MRR annualised |
| **ARPU** | Average revenue per active subscriber |
| **Active** | Subscriptions currently billing |
| **In trial** | Started, not yet charged |
| **Cancelled** | Ended in the period |
| **Logo churn** | The share of customers lost over the window, by count rather than by value |

::note{title="MRR excludes usage and one-off charges"}
It is *committed* recurring revenue: what a customer is contracted to pay each month. Metered
usage and one-off charges are real revenue but not committed, so including them would make MRR
move with consumption and stop being a planning number.

Look at invoices and transactions for total collected.
::

## Multiple currencies

MRR is shown per currency, because ₹1 and $1 are not the same number and adding them is how a
dashboard starts lying.

If you want one roll-up figure, set reporting exchange rates under **Settings → Currencies**. Those
rates only affect reporting — they never change what a customer is charged.

## What to watch

**Past due** is the money you have earned and not collected. It should be small and it should move
— a number that only grows means your retry schedule or your emails are not working. See
[Recover failed payments](/help/guides/recover-failed-payments).

**Cancellations with reasons** tell you why customers leave, but only if your team records a reason
when they cancel. It costs one click and it is the difference between knowing and guessing.
**Reports → Cancellations** leads with what each cancellation cost: MRR lost by month or week, why
paying customers left, how long they stayed, the reasons given and the largest losses. Trials that
never converted are counted separately, so they don't inflate your paying churn.

**Trials not converting** shows up as a healthy in-trial count and flat active count.

## Going deeper

Every list — customers, subscriptions, invoices, transactions — can be filtered and sorted, and
the numbers on the metrics screens link through to the rows behind them. When a figure looks
wrong, click it and read the underlying records rather than re-deriving it yourself.
