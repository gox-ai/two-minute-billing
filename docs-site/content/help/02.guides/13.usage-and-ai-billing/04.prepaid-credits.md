Prepaid credit lets a customer pay for usage before they use it. They buy credit, or you give it
to them, and the usage that the credit covers costs them nothing more on the invoice. Each lot of
credit is a **credit grant**: an amount for one customer, in money or in your own credit unit,
with an expiry date if you set one.

Credit pays the **usage lines** of a subscription's invoices. It does not pay the plan fee,
one-time charges or an invoice that you create by hand.

## Name your own credit unit

Skip this to sell credit in money ("USD 10.00 of credit"). A credit unit lets you sell
"1,000 credits" instead.

::steps

### Create the unit

Open **Settings → Credit units** and select **Create credit unit**. Give it a **Name** and a
**Plural name**, for example "credit" and "credits". We make the **Unit ID** from the name, and
you cannot change it later.

### Set its value

Select the **Currency** and the **Value of one unit**. A value of 0.01 makes 100 credits worth
1.00. **Decimal places** (0 to 4) sets how finely you can give the unit: 0 gives whole units only.

::

The first grant in a unit locks its currency, value and decimal places. After that, you can change
only the names. **Archive** a unit to stop new grants in it. Customers keep the credit they have
and can still use it.

::note
Credit in your own unit is always taxed at purchase. "Taxed at use" is for credit in money only.
::

## Give credit by hand

Open the customer, select **View credits** on the **Credits** card, then **Give credit**.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Type of credit** | _Promotional_ is free for the customer and starts at once. _Paid_ creates an invoice for the credit. |
| **Unit** | **Money**, or one of your credit units. |
| **Amount** | How much credit to give. |
| **Price, before tax** | Paid credit only. What the customer pays. It is the cost basis of the credit. |
| **Tax** | Paid credit only. **Taxed at purchase** or **Taxed at use**. See [Credit and tax](#credit-and-tax). |
| **Priority** | 0 to 100, default 50. Credit with a lower number is used first. |
| **Expires on** | Leave empty for no expiry. |

Paid credit always has an invoice. Select **Create invoice**, and the credit starts when the
invoice is paid. If you already have the money, select **Mark the invoice as paid** and enter the
**Payment method**, a **Reference** and the date in **Paid on**: the invoice is paid and the
credit is active in the same step. Either way, the drawer ends with a link to the invoice.

To give paid credit, your role needs permission to manage invoices as well as credits.

## Sell credit packs

A credit pack is a one-time price that gives paid credit when its invoice is paid.

::steps

### Create a one-time price

In **Product Catalog**, open an add-on (**Addons**) or a product in **One-time products** and select
**Add price**. Make it a one-time price. A plan's one-time price cannot be a credit pack.

### Turn on the pack

In the **Grants credit** card, turn on **Sell this price as a credit pack**. Set the **Unit**, the
amount in the pack (multiplied by the quantity bought), the **Priority**, **Expires after** (days
after the payment, or empty for no expiry) and **Tax on the credit**.

### Sell it

The customer buys the pack through checkout, a payment link, an invoice that you create, or the
**Buy credits** list in the customer portal. The portal lists your packs with the credit each one
gives. **Buy** opens checkout, and we add the credit when the customer pays.

::

The price can be less than the credit it gives. A pack that sells 1,000 credits worth USD 10.00 for
USD 9.00 has a cost basis of USD 9.00. A pack invoice never uses credit itself, so free credit
cannot buy paid credit.

**Purchase limits** in **Settings → Credits** set the minimum and maximum value of the packs on one
purchase, per currency, through checkout, payment links, the portal and manual invoices. Credit
that you give by hand has no limit.

## Include credits in a plan

On a recurring price of a plan, turn on **Give credit each billing period** in the **Grants credit**
card. Set the amount each period (multiplied by the quantity on the subscription) and the
**Priority**. Plan credit is promotional: it is part of the plan fee, so it has no cost basis.

The first period's credit arrives when the first period is billed. A trial gives no credit: the
credit starts with the first paid period. Each period's credit ends with that period.

Under **At the end of the billing period**, select what happens to credit that is not used:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Unused credit expires** | Each period starts with the new credit only. |
| **Carry unused credit to the next period** | A part of the unused credit moves to a new grant. |
| **Part to carry** | 1 to 100 percent of what is left, default 100. |
| **Maximum to carry** | The most that moves. Leave empty for no maximum. |
| **Carried credit lasts** | **A number of periods** (default 1, the end of the next period), or **Until it is used**. |

Carried credit does not move a second time. It lasts until its own expiry.

For example, a plan gives 1,000 credits each month, carries 50% with a maximum of 150, for one
period. The customer uses 600. Of the 400 left, 50% is 200, and the maximum makes it 150. The next
month starts with 1,000 new credits and 150 carried credits that expire at the end of that month.

### Renew credits on their own clock

Plan credit can renew more often than the bill. A plan billed yearly can still give 25 credits
each month. Under **Renews**, select how often:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Each billing term** | One grant for each term, as above. This is the default. |
| **Every** N **days**, **weeks**, **months**, **quarters**, **half years** or **years** | One grant for each window. A window cannot be longer than the billing term. |
| **Never** | One grant at the start. It stays until it is used or the subscription ends. It does not carry over. |

Credits do not renew every hour. Use an allowance on an entitlement for an hourly limit.

Under **Windows start**, select where each window begins:

- **From the subscription start**: windows start at the start of the billing term. The last window
  of a term ends at the term end.
- **On the calendar**: on the hour, at midnight, on Monday, or on the 1st of the month, quarter,
  half year or year, in the business time zone. A first window that starts before the
  subscription gets the full amount, unless your billing settings say otherwise.

Each window's credit pays only the usage inside that window, also on a yearly invoice. Daily
credit for 2 May does not pay usage from 3 May. The invoice line stays one line.

**Carry unused credit** works for each window: the part moves to the next window, and **A number
of renewals** counts windows. The customer portal and the access check show when the credit renews
next (`renews_at`).

Your billing settings decide what happens to window credit when the plan changes, when the
subscription pauses, and when it is set to cancel at the end of the term:

| Event | Default |
|---|---|
| Upgrade | The new plan's credit at once, in full. The old window's credit ends. |
| Downgrade | The old credit runs to the end of its window. The new plan's credit starts with the next window. |
| Pause | No window that starts during the pause gets credit. |
| Cancel at the end of the term | A window that starts before the end gets credit. Nothing after the end. |

When your billing settings move the windows on by the paused time, the window that is open at the
pause runs on for the paused time: its credit expires that much later. From the subscription
start, every later window moves too. On the calendar, only the next window moves: it starts when
the extended window ends and stops at the next calendar boundary, so it is shorter. The short
window follows the same rule as a partial first window: the full amount, an amount prorated for its
part of the calendar window (rounded to the nearest whole step of the unit), or nothing until the next full window. After it, the windows are on
the calendar again. A pause that ends past a calendar
boundary skips that boundary. A resume that starts a new term starts the windows again from that
term.

An allowance on an entitlement can renew with these credits: see
[Renew an allowance](/help/developers/concepts/entitlements#renew-an-allowance).

With the API, set `reset` on the price's `grants_credit`:

```json
{
  "kind": "per_cycle",
  "unit": "credit",
  "amount": 25,
  "reset": { "every": "month", "count": 1, "anchor": "subscription" }
}
```

## How credit applies to an invoice

When an invoice is finalized, grants pay its usage lines in this order:

1. Restored credit (credit given back from an earlier invoice).
2. The lowest **Priority**.
3. The soonest expiry. Credit with no expiry goes last.
4. Promotional before paid.
5. The oldest grant.

Usage that no grant covers bills as usual. Credit with an expiry date still pays for usage from
before that date, on the invoice that bills it.

### Credit and tax

Promotional credit and credit that is **Taxed at purchase** apply **before tax**: they lower the
amount that tax is charged on. For paid credit taxed at purchase, the tax was already on the pack
invoice.

Credit that is **Taxed at use** applies **after tax**. Its pack invoice has no tax. On the usage
invoice, the credit pays the rest of the usage and the tax on it, as money. We never gross up the
tax.

Example, with 20% tax. A customer has USD 20.00 of promotional credit and a USD 100.00 pack taxed
at use. They use USD 50.00:

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Usage | USD 50.00 |
| Promotional credit, before tax | − USD 20.00 |
| Taxed amount | USD 30.00 |
| Tax at 20% | USD 6.00 |
| Paid credit, after tax | − USD 36.00 |
| **Amount due** | **USD 0.00** |

The pack has USD 64.00 left. The promotional credit paid no tax, so the tax is USD 6.00, not
USD 10.00.

Set the default for paid credit in **Settings → Credits → Default tax treatment for paid credit**.
With **Not set**, each paid credit pack must set its own.

## Stop usage when credit runs out

By default, usage never stops for lack of credit: what credit does not cover goes on the invoice.
To stop it, open **Settings → Credits** and turn on **Block usage when credit is used up**.

Blocking acts on the access check: the call your app makes before each request. When the
customer has no credit available, the check answers that the request is not allowed, and your app
does not make the request. Usage that your app already sent is always billed.

With blocking on, the first of these sets the policy for a customer:

1. The customer's override, until the date it ends.
2. The policy on a credit grant (**When this credit is used up**).
3. The policy on the plan price.
4. The **Default policy** in **Settings → Credits**: **Bill usage** (the default) or **Block usage**.

The **Overdraft limit** lets the balance go below zero before we block, per currency. 0 blocks at
zero.

### Set an override for one customer

On the customer's **Credits** page, select **Edit** on **Settings for this customer**. Select
**Block usage** or **Bill usage**, and a date in **Override ends on** if the override is for a
time only, such as a trial of a higher volume. You can also set an **Overdraft limit** for this
customer, for example a trusted customer who can go USD 2.00 below zero. Leave it empty to use the
business limit. These controls show only while blocking is on.

The **Available credit** card shows the policy that applies now and what sets it.

## Holds

When many requests run at the same time, your app can place a **hold** before each one. The hold
reserves the estimated cost, so two requests cannot spend the same credit. When the request ends,
your app captures the hold with the real usage, or releases it.

The customer's **Credits** page lists **Open holds** with their amount, meter and expiry. An open
hold counts against the balance. To free one by hand, select **Release hold**. The app cannot
capture it after that. Releasing needs permission to hold, capture and release credit.

In **Settings → Credits**:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Hold time** | 60 seconds | How long a hold stays open when the app does not set a time. 1 to 86,400 seconds. |
| **Open holds limit** | 50% of the balance | The part of the balance that open holds can use at one time. |

The open holds limit and the "no credit" refusal apply only when the policy is **Block usage**.
With **Bill usage**, a hold only records the reservation.

## Take credit back, or give it back

**Void a promotional grant.** On the customer's **Credits** page, select **Void grant** on the
grant. The customer loses what is left. You cannot undo this.

**Refund a paid grant.** Paid credit leaves only through a credit note on the invoice that sold
it. A credit note on a pack line removes the unused part of the credit in proportion, up to the
part not used yet.

**Void an invoice that credit paid.** The credit goes back to the grant it came from. If that
grant has expired, the credit goes to a new grant of the same kind, which is used first and
expires 12 months later.

**Refund or credit an invoice that credit paid.** The credit's share comes back as credit, never
as cash. The credit note shows it as **Credit Grants Returned**.

## See the balance and every movement

The customer's **Credits** page has:

- **Available credit**: the credit left, less unbilled usage that credit pays and open holds, plus
  any overdraft limit. The invoice shows the final amounts.
- **Credit grants**: each grant with its category, what remains, its cost basis, its expiry and
  its source (**Purchase**, **Plan credits**, **Given by hand**, **Restored**).
- **Transactions**: each movement, newest first: **Granted**, **Activated**, **Applied to
  invoice**, **Expired**, **Voided**, **Restored**, **Rolled over in** and **Rolled over out**.
  Filter by grant, movement, category, unit, invoice and date.

In the customer portal, the **Credits** section shows the customer their credit, each grant with
its expiry, the **Buy credits** list and their **Credit history**.
