Two Minute Billing charges saved cards through **your** Checkout.com account. When a customer
pays an invoice now, the payment form also shows Google Pay if Checkout.com enables it for your
account. Apple Pay on the Checkout.com Hosted Payments Page is coming soon. You keep your Checkout.com rates, and Checkout.com pays out to your bank as usual.

## Before you start

- A Checkout.com account. Use the sandbox to test; live payments need a live account.
- Checkout.com gives you **sandbox** keys (`sk_sbox_…`, `pk_sbox_…`) and **live** keys. Connect the
  sandbox keys to a test business and the live keys to a live business. Two Minute Billing
  refuses a mix.
- You need the Owner or Admin role in Checkout.com to read the entity ID.
- Every customer who saves a card needs an email address. Checkout.com refuses a card without
  one.
- For Google Pay: a Google Pay Merchant ID from the
  [Google Pay & Wallet Console](https://pay.google.com/business/console). Checkout.com enables
  Google Pay when you send it to your account manager.
- For Apple Pay: Checkout.com must enable the Hosted Payments Page and Apple Pay on it for your
  account. Ask your account manager or solutions engineer.
- You need permission to manage payment gateways in Two Minute Billing. The owner and admin
  roles have it.

You need six values:

| Field | Where to find it | Looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Secret key** | Checkout.com Dashboard → Developers → Keys. Use sk_sbox_ keys for a test business and live keys for a live business. | `sk_…` |
| **Public key** | — | `pk_…` |
| **Processing channel ID** | Checkout.com Dashboard → Settings → Processing channels. | `pc_…` |
| **API prefix** | The 8 characters before .api.checkout.com in your base URL. Find it in Checkout.com Dashboard → Developers → Overview. You can also paste the full base URL. | `abcd1234` |
| **Default billing country** | Checkout.com needs a billing country to open the card form. We use the country of the customer's billing address. If the customer has no billing address, we use this two-letter country code. | `US` |
| **Entity ID** | The ID of the Checkout.com entity that owns the processing channel (ent_…). We need it to register the webhook. In the Checkout.com Dashboard, click your account name at the top left, select the entity in the Entity column and click Switch view. Then click the Settings icon and select Account details. You must have the Owner or Admin role. | `ent_…` |
<!-- /generated:keys -->

::warning
**Keep the secret key secret.** Anyone with the secret key can charge and refund in your Checkout.com account. Paste it only into
Two Minute Billing, which stores it encrypted, and never into an email or a chat.
::

## Permissions

::warning
**Check the secret key's scopes.** A Checkout.com secret key can do only what its **scopes** allow. A key with the **Default**
scopes works. If you made the key with **custom** scopes, it needs every scope below. A missing
scope does not show when you connect: it shows later, when a refund or the webhook fails.
::

| Scope | Two Minute Billing uses it to |
| --- | --- |
| `gateway` (payments, payment details, captures, refunds, voids) | Charge, capture, cancel, refund and find payments |
| `payment-sessions` | Show the card form to your customers |
| `vault` (customers, instruments) | Make customers, and read and remove saved cards |
| `flow:workflows` | Register the webhook for you |

If you made the **public key** with custom scopes, it needs `payment-sessions:pay` and
`vault-tokenization`: the payment form and Google Pay use them in the customer's browser.

Apple Pay opens the Hosted Payments Page (`/hosted-payments`) with the secret key. Checkout.com's
API reference names no extra scope for it; Checkout.com turns the page on for your account.

Two Minute Billing does not call the disputes API, so the `disputes` scopes are not necessary.
To read the **Entity ID**, you need the **Owner** or **Admin** role in the Checkout.com Dashboard.

## Connect it in Two Minute Billing

1. Open **Settings → Payment gateways** and click **Add gateway**.
2. Select **Checkout.com** and paste the six values from the table above.
3. Select the **currencies** and the **payment methods** this account handles.
4. Click **Create gateway account**.

Then open the account and click **Check credentials**. **Healthy** means Checkout.com accepted the
secret key on your API prefix.

The payment methods you can turn on for Checkout.com:

<!-- generated:methods -->
- **Cards** — Pay now also shows Google Pay. To enable it, send your Google Pay Merchant ID to your Checkout.com account manager. Apple Pay is coming soon.
<!-- /generated:methods -->

### Add Google Pay

Google Pay shows only when a customer pays an invoice now. It is a card payment to
Checkout.com, so refunds and disputes work as they do for cards. Two Minute Billing does not
save Google Pay for renewals: to pay renewals automatically, the customer saves a card.

1. In the [Google Pay & Wallet Console](https://pay.google.com/business/console), copy your
   Google Pay Merchant ID.
2. Send it to your Checkout.com account manager and ask them to enable Google Pay for your
   processing channel.

You do not change anything in Two Minute Billing. When Checkout.com enables Google Pay, the
payment form shows it.

### Add Apple Pay

**Coming soon.** Apple Pay on Checkout.com is not available yet. We did not complete its tests.

Apple Pay shows only when a customer pays an invoice now, in a browser that can pay with Apple
Pay (Safari with a card in Apple Wallet). The customer selects **Apple Pay**, pays on the
Checkout.com Hosted Payments Page and comes back to the portal. Your domain does not need Apple
Pay verification: the page is on Checkout.com's domain.

1. Ask your Checkout.com account manager to enable the Hosted Payments Page for your processing
   channel.
2. Ask them to enable Apple Pay on the Hosted Payments Page.

::note
**Apple Pay is for one payment.** Two Minute Billing does not save Apple Pay for renewals: to pay
renewals automatically, the customer saves a card. The payment shows as **Apple Pay** under
**Paid with**, and refunds and disputes work as they do for cards.
::

## Webhooks

Checkout.com tells Two Minute Billing about things that happen after a charge: a capture or a
refund that completes later, a payment that is returned, a dispute, a saved card that the card
network updates.

<!-- generated:webhooks -->
Two Minute Billing registers the webhook with Checkout.com for you when you save the account. Nothing to copy.

If the gateway account page shows the webhook as not configured:

1. Click “Register with Checkout.com” on the gateway account page. We create the webhook workflow and keep its signature key.
2. Optional: make sure that it shows under Developers → Webhooks in Checkout.com.
<!-- /generated:webhooks -->

The workflow sends only the events of the processing channel on the gateway account.

## Test it

In your test business, create a customer with an email address and save the test card
`4242 4242 4242 4242` with any future expiry date and any three-digit CVV. If the 3D Secure test
page opens, enter the password `Checkout1!`. Collect an invoice: it is marked paid, and **Logs**
shows the call to Checkout.com and Checkout.com's answer.

To test Google Pay, sign in to a Google account in Chrome and join Google's
[test card suite](https://developers.google.com/pay/api/web/guides/resources/test-card-suite).
Open an invoice in the customer portal, click **Pay now** and select **Google Pay**. Select a
test card in the Google Pay sheet. The invoice is marked paid. Google Pay is a card payment, so
the transaction shows **Card** under **Paid with**.

To test Apple Pay, use Safari on a device that has an Apple
[sandbox tester account](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/sandbox-testing/) with one of
Apple's test cards in Wallet. Open an invoice in the customer portal, click **Pay now**, select
**Apple Pay** and click **Continue**. Pay on the Checkout.com page. You come back to the portal,
and the invoice is marked paid.

## Go live

1. In your live Checkout.com account, copy the live **secret key** and **public key**, the live
   **processing channel ID**, the live **API prefix** and the live **entity ID**.
2. In your **live** business, add Checkout.com with those values.
3. Two Minute Billing creates a separate webhook workflow for live. Check it under
   **Developers → Webhooks** in Checkout.com.
4. For Google Pay: make sure that Checkout.com enabled Google Pay on the live account, and
   request production access in the Google Pay & Wallet Console.
5. For Apple Pay: make sure that Checkout.com enabled the Hosted Payments Page and Apple Pay on
   the live account.

## Troubleshoot

- **Check credentials says "We could not check the credentials." with "Checkout.com refused the
  secret key".** The secret key is wrong, its scopes do not include the APIs Two Minute Billing
  uses, or the API prefix is wrong. Copy the key and the prefix again and update the gateway
  account.
- **Check credentials says "These are Checkout.com live keys. A test business needs sandbox
  keys".** Copy the sandbox keys (`sk_sbox_…`, `pk_sbox_…`), or connect the live keys to a live
  business. A live business shows the opposite message.
- **Check credentials says "Checkout.com entity_id must look like ent_ followed by 26 letters and
  digits".** Copy the entity ID again from **Account details**, as the table above describes.
- **Saving a card fails with "Checkout.com needs the customer's email address to save a card."**
  Add an email address to the customer, then try again.
- **The payment form shows no Google Pay.** Checkout.com has not enabled Google Pay for your
  account, or the customer's browser has no Google Pay wallet. Google Pay never shows when a
  customer saves a card.
- **Pay now shows no Apple Pay.** The customer's browser cannot pay with Apple Pay (it is not
  Safari, or Apple Wallet has no card). Apple Pay never shows when a customer saves a card.
- **The customer sees "a problem with the payment configuration" after selecting Apple Pay.**
  Checkout.com refused the Hosted Payments Page. **Logs** shows its answer. Ask Checkout.com to
  enable the Hosted Payments Page and Apple Pay on it.
