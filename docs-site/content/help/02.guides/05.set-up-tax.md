Two Minute Billing works out tax on every invoice line, from where your customer is and where you are registered. That is why a customer's billing address matters. You set up each place you charge tax once, under **Settings → Taxes**.

## Add the places you charge tax

Add each country you are registered in. Within it, add the states or provinces where the rules are
sub-national.

For each, set the rates. Some places need more than one rate on the same sale — an Indian domestic
sale carries CGST and SGST separately, while an inter-state sale carries IGST instead. Both print
separately on the invoice, because that is what the document has to show.

Record your registration numbers — in India, the GSTIN for each state you are registered in. Which
one applies follows from where you are registered relative to your customer.

## Decide whether prices include tax

| Setting | Your price of 100 means | Your customer pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Exclusive** | 100 before tax | 100 plus tax |
| **Inclusive** | 100 including tax | 100 |

Select the one that matches how you advertise. Most business-to-business sellers quote exclusive.
Most consumer sellers quote inclusive.

## Tax different products differently

A digital service and a physical good are often taxed differently in the same country. Create a
tax profile for each class of product and set which profile a product uses.

## Handle exemptions

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Customer is tax exempt | Mark them exempt on their customer record |
| Business customer in another country | Record their VAT number — reverse charge applies where the rules allow, and the obligation moves to them |
| Customer withholds tax and pays it themselves | Set their withholding rate. The invoice shows the full amount and the withheld part separately, so the difference is explained rather than looking like a short payment |

::tip{title="Validate VAT numbers, do not trust them"}
Two Minute Billing can check a customer's VAT number against the official register. A wrong number
means the tax you did not charge is tax you owe.
::

## Each invoice keeps its own tax

When an invoice is issued, it records the rates, your registration and the place of supply as they
were that day.

Changing a customer's address or a rate afterwards does not re-tax a document that already exists.
New invoices use the new setup. That is what makes an old invoice still defensible a year
later.
