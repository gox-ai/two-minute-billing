Two Minute Billing hosts two pages for your customers, both carrying your branding.

| Page | Your customer can |
|---|---|
| **Checkout** | Select a plan, enter their details and pay |
| **Portal** | Update their card, see invoices, and whatever else you allow |

Card details go straight to your payment provider. They never reach your servers, which keeps you
out of the compliance scope that storing card numbers would put you in.

## Set what customers can do

**Settings → Customer portal** controls the portal. Every switch you turn on is a support ticket your
team stops receiving. Every switch you leave off is a job they keep doing.

Worth enabling for most businesses: updating a payment method, downloading invoices, and seeing
what is owed. Whether customers can change plan or cancel on their own is a commercial decision
rather than a technical one.

## Send a customer a link

Generate the link from the customer or subscription, and send it to them.

::warning{title="The link is single use and expires in an hour"}
Opening it spends it. So do not email the same link twice, and do not put it behind a link checker
or a mail scanner that follows links — the scanner opens it and your customer gets an expired
page.

Generate a fresh one whenever a customer asks. It costs nothing.
::

## Show them inside your app

Checkout can also open inside your own website or app: as a popup over your page, or inline in
your layout. The portal opens as a popup. Your customer never leaves your app.

1. Under **Settings → Checkout → Embed on your website**, add each website that may show them,
   for example `https://app.example.com`. Use https. A test business also accepts
   `http://localhost`. You can add up to 20.
2. Copy **Code for your page** from the same card and give it to your developer, with
   [Embed checkout and the portal in your app](/help/developers/recipes/embed-checkout-and-portal).

When you remove a website, checkout and the portal stop opening there from the next page load.
The usage widget uses the same list.

## Choose what checkout asks for

**Settings → Checkout and portal fields** sets, for each field, whether checkout and the portal
ask for it: **Required**, **Optional** or **Hidden**. The portal also has **Read-only**. Ask for less
and more customers finish. Some fields stay required for a reason:

| When | What stays |
|---|---|
| Tax is set up | Country is required |
| GST is set up | State is required |
| A Stripe account in India takes your payments | Country is required, and first name and address line 1 stay visible |

With no tax and no such Stripe account, you can hide everything except the email.

When you hide address line 1, checkout and the portal hide lines 2 and 3 too.

### Customers outside India on an Indian Stripe account

Stripe in India refuses a card from outside India unless the customer gives a name and a street
address. So checkout asks those customers for address line 1.

To ask for less, turn on **Settings → Checkout → Fill the street for buyers outside India**. When a
customer outside India leaves address line 1 empty, we send their city and state to Stripe as
line 1. The postal code fills both, so checkout then asks for the postal code instead of the
street, and you can hide address line 1. Your invoices show what the customer entered.

::warning{title="Stripe reports this address"}
Stripe uses these details to report the export to its banks, and it can make its check stricter
at any time. Turn the setting on only if you accept that Stripe receives the city and state in
place of a street.
::

## Brand them

Under **Settings**, set your logo, icon and brand colour. Check the preview before you send anyone
a link — these pages are where a customer hands over a card, and one that does not look like you
is one they abandon.
