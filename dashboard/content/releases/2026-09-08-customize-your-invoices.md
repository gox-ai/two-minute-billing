---
title: Decide what goes on your invoices
date: 2026-09-08
type: Feature
tags: Invoices, Credit notes, Settings
summary: Line items now show units and unit price, and you choose your invoice title, number format, which fields appear, your address layout and a signature.
---

Your invoices and credit notes now follow your rules. Pick what they're called, how they're
numbered, which fields they carry, and sign them off.

- **Call it what your tax authority expects.** Invoices can print as `INVOICE` or `TAX INVOICE`;
  credit notes as `CREDIT NOTE` or `REFUND`. Settings → Invoices, and Settings → Credit notes.
- **Number them your way.** Add a date stamp to the number — the year, the fiscal year, a month or
  a full date, in thirteen formats — choose the separators, and set how far the counter steps. The
  screen shows you the actual next number as you type, so you can see the result before you save.
  The counter always moves forward, so changing the format is safe.
- **Show only the fields you want.** Customer ID, subscription ID, billing address and tax number,
  invoice status, billing period, next billing date. Plus three you can now switch **on**: the
  shipping address, the credit notes raised against an invoice, and up to three of your own custom
  fields.
- **Lay out your address to suit your letterhead.** Expanded over several lines, collapsed to one,
  or a template you write yourself — previewed with your own address in it.
- **Sign your documents.** Add a signature line and an image, and choose which bottom corner they
  sit in — bottom right or bottom left. The block is pinned to the foot of the page, so it lands in
  the same place whether the invoice has two lines or twenty. Settings → Documents.
- **Tell offline payers where to send the money.** Build bank transfer or PayPal blocks with your
  own labels and values, and print them on your invoices. You can match them to the invoice
  currency or the customer's country, so a customer paying in euros isn't shown your rupee account.
  Settings → Payment instructions.

**Your line items now show their maths.** Every invoice gains a **Units** and a **Unit price**
column, so a line reads `5 × ₹940.00 = ₹4,700.00` instead of just the total. Nothing to switch on —
it is on every invoice and credit note from now on.

Everything else about your existing documents is untouched until you change something. Every setting starts at exactly
what your invoices already do, so nothing shifts under you the day this arrives.

One correction worth calling out: a tax registration number was previously labelled "GSTIN" on
every invoice, whichever country you sell from. It now says GSTIN in India, VAT across the EU and
the UK, and "Tax ID" elsewhere — so if you sell outside India, that caption on your documents is
now the right one.
